## Question
"Tell me about a time you got critical feedback. How did you react, and what did you change?"

## Interview Answer
In my first few months at [company], my [lead / senior developer] reviewed a large PR of mine for [feature] and left [N] comments. The overall feedback was blunt: the PR was too big to review properly, and I'd [e.g. "mixed refactoring with the feature" / "accepted AI-generated code that I couldn't fully explain"]. My first reaction was to defend it, because the feature worked. But I asked for a short call to understand their view, and they walked me through how hard it was to spot bugs in [1,500] changed lines. They were right. So I changed how I work. I now split work into small PRs of [under ~300 lines] with a clear description, keep refactors separate, and I review every AI-generated change line by line before raising a PR, and write tests for the risky parts. Over the next [quarter], my PRs [got approved faster / review rounds dropped from X to Y], and later my lead asked me to [review others' PRs / help write our PR guidelines].

## Explanation
### What the interviewer is really assessing
- **Coachability.** Teams want people who absorb feedback and improve, not people who argue or sulk.
- **Self-awareness.** Can you admit something real without getting defensive?
- **Behaviour change.** Did the feedback lead to a lasting change in how you work?

### STAR template
- **Situation** — Who gave it, in what context, and what exactly they said: [person/role], [context], [the feedback].
- **Task** — What you needed to do: understand it, fix the immediate issue, and stop it recurring.
- **Action** — Your reaction (honest, including the first impulse if you handled it well), how you clarified, the specific changes: [asked questions], [new habit / process], [how you checked progress].
- **Result** — Evidence of change: [metric], [later feedback], [extra responsibility you were given].

### Story ideas from your background
- Code review feedback on PR size, naming, missing tests, or over-relying on AI-generated code.
- Trainer feedback: learner reviews saying sessions were too fast, or too theory-heavy, and how you restructured them.
- Feedback on communication: not raising blockers early, not updating the ticket.
- A design review that called out accessibility gaps in your UI.

### Variants of the question
- "What's the most useful feedback you've ever received?"
- "Tell me about a time you were wrong."
- "How do you handle criticism of your code in reviews?"

## Follow-ups
- **What if you disagree with the feedback?** — Understand it fully first, then share your reasoning with evidence. If it's still a judgement call, defer to the team standard and move on.
- **How do you give feedback to others?** — Specific, about the code not the person, with a reason and a suggestion; ask questions rather than issue verdicts.
- **What feedback are you working on right now?** — Have a real, current one ready.

## Common Mistakes
- A fake weakness ("they said I work too hard").
- Feedback that was clearly unfair, so the story is really about the other person.
- No proof of change. "I took it on board" isn't a result.

## Tips
- Admitting your first defensive impulse, and then what you did instead, makes the story believable.
- The AI-code review angle is current and honest for someone who uses agents daily, and it shows maturity about the tools.
