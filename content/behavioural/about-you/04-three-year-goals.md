## Question
"Where do you see yourself in three (or five) years?" Variants: "What are your career goals?", "What does growth look like for you?"

## Interview Answer
In three years I want to be a strong senior-leaning frontend engineer, someone the team trusts to own a large feature or area end to end: the UI, the API contract, performance, and the trade-offs. Concretely, I'd like to be the person who [e.g. "leads the design system" / "owns web performance for the product"], and be comfortable enough on the backend with [Node, Postgres, caching] to build full features without handing off every API change. I also want to keep the teaching side I built as a trainer, so mentoring newer developers, running knowledge sessions, and writing good internal docs. In the first year here, that means learning your codebase and standards deeply and shipping reliably. Later it means taking on bigger, more ambiguous problems. I'd rather grow into that inside one good team than keep switching, which is part of why I'm being selective about this move.

## Explanation
### What the interviewer is really assessing
- **Ambition with realism** — Do you want to grow, and is your plan plausible for 3 years?
- **Fit and retention** — Does your goal exist inside this company? "I want to start my own startup" or "I want to move into data science" tells them you will leave.
- **Self-direction** — Do you have a plan, or are you waiting to be told?

### Template
- **Situation** — Where you are now: [~1 year as React/Next.js dev + 1.5 years training].
- **Task (the goal)** — One clear role-level goal: [senior frontend / frontend owner of X / full-stack product engineer]. Describe it by *responsibilities*, not titles.
- **Action (the path)** — 2–3 concrete steps: [deepen architecture & performance], [build backend competence], [mentor / lead initiatives].
- **Result (link to them)** — How this role is the right next step on that path, and that you want to grow *here*.

### Story ideas from your background
- Your trainer experience → future mentoring / tech-lead direction.
- Side projects with Postgres/Redis/Firebase → full-stack ownership goal.
- Your AI-agent workflow → "engineer who designs and reviews, not only types code".

### Variants of the question
- "What kind of engineer do you want to become?"
- "Do you see yourself in management?"
- "What do you want to learn in the next year?"

## Follow-ups
- **Do you want to go into management?** — At 2–3 years, say you want to grow technically first, while enjoying mentoring; you'll decide on management once you've seen what tech lead work is like.
- **Frontend or full-stack?** — Frontend-strong with full-stack ability. Match the role's emphasis, and stay honest.
- **How will you get there?** — Name concrete actions: owning features, reading code of senior engineers, a learning plan, feedback from your manager.

## Common Mistakes
- "In your seat" jokes or "I don't know" — both read as unprepared.
- Goals unrelated to the role (switching domains, starting a company, going abroad soon).
- Title chasing ("I want to be a lead in 2 years") without describing the skills.
- Being so vague it says nothing ("growing as a developer").

## Tips
- Talk in terms of **scope and impact**, not titles: "own a whole area", "make decisions others rely on".
- Close by tying the goal back to this company; it signals you intend to stay.
- Keep it under 60 seconds.
