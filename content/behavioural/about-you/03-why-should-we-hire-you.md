## Question
"Why should we hire you?" usually comes paired with "Why do you want to work here?" Answer both: what you bring to them, and why them specifically.

## Interview Answer
Three reasons. First, I can contribute on the frontend from week one. I've spent the last year shipping production React and Next.js at [company], including [one strong example, e.g. "migrating our pages to the App Router" or "building the reporting dashboard used by N clients"]. Second, I bring something most developers at my level don't: a year and a half as a trainer, explaining React and web fundamentals to [students and business owners]. In practice that means clear PRs, good documentation, and I'm comfortable onboarding or pairing with people. Third, I learn fast and I'm not limited to the UI. I've built [side project] with [Postgres / Redis / Firebase], and I use AI coding agents in a disciplined way, so I can pick up unfamiliar parts of the stack quickly. As for why here: [specific reason, e.g. "your product has real scale and performance problems on the frontend" / "I read your post on X and that's exactly the kind of engineering I want to learn"]. And the role's mix of [what the JD emphasises] matches where I'm strongest and where I want to grow.

## Explanation
### What the interviewer is really assessing
- **Self-awareness** — Do you know your actual strengths, or just list adjectives ("hard-working, passionate")?
- **Fit with the job description** — Did you map your strengths to *their* needs?
- **Genuine interest** — Did you research them, or would you say this to any company?

### Template
This is a pitch, built from evidence:

- **Situation (their need)** — From the JD and your research: [the 2–3 things the role needs most, e.g. "Next.js performance", "design system work", "full-stack ownership"].
- **Task (your claim)** — Pick 3 strengths that match those needs. Not more.
- **Action (evidence)** — For each strength, one concrete proof: [project, number, behaviour]. Claims without proof are ignored.
- **Result (why them)** — One or two specific reasons about the company: product, scale, engineering culture, tech blog, open source, a problem you would enjoy.

### Strengths you can honestly claim, with proof to fill
| Strength | Proof to fill in |
|---|---|
| Production React/Next.js | [feature shipped, metric, technical decision you made] |
| Communication and mentoring | [N learners trained, onboarding a teammate, docs you wrote] |
| Fast learner, full-stack curious | [side project stack, something learned in [N] weeks] |
| Productive with AI tools | [how you plan, review and test AI-generated code; time saved] |

### Variants of the question
- "What makes you different from other candidates?"
- "What do you know about our company?"
- "What would you bring to this team?"
- "Why this role and not a backend or full-stack role?"

## Follow-ups
- **What's your biggest weakness then?** — Pick a real, non-fatal one with a fix in progress, e.g. "less experience with large-scale systems; I'm closing that with [project / reading / this role]".
- **What do you know about our product?** — Mention something you actually used or read. Have one question ready about it.
- **Other candidates have more years of experience. Why you?** — Years differ from depth of exposure: training meant reviewing and debugging hundreds of codebases, and you have shipped production code; offer evidence, not argument.

## Common Mistakes
- Generic adjectives without evidence ("I'm passionate and a team player").
- Talking only about what *you* want from them (learning, growth) and never about what *they* get.
- Company flattery that could apply anywhere ("you're a great company with a great culture").
- Overselling AI usage in a way that sounds like you can't code without it.

## Tips
- Before each interview, write three lines: their top need → your matching proof. That's your answer.
- "Three reasons" is a good structure: it's easy to follow and stops you rambling.
- One specific detail about the company (a product feature, an engineering blog post, a talk) beats five generic compliments.
